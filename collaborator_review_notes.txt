## Analyses I'd like you to run

These are ordered roughly by how much each one changes the argument relative to its cost.

**Cheap, from outputs you already have**

- **Study-level decomposition on CQ500.** Classify every urgent miss as a perception, decoding or reasoning miss. This is Fig. 1b and directly parallels their 21 of 155.
- **A zero-parameter urgency score.** For each study, take the maximum diagnostic-head score across the labels that map to the critical-findings list. Use it directly as a triage score, with no LLM screener, and plot its sensitivity against flag rate next to the prose pipeline's single point. This is your VaxSeer-style simplest adequate baseline, and it may be the most persuasive panel in the paper.
- **Critical-list coverage.** Map the ASNR critical findings list (their ref. 17) onto the 82 CT labels and report what fraction is covered. This needs no GPU and defines the scope of the claim.
- **Secondary-finding stratification.** For decoding misses versus correctly reported urgent findings, compare how often the report named another abnormality. This tests the concise-summary mechanism.
- **Calibration of external scores.** Make reliability diagrams for the head on CQ500 and on the in-house cohort. This lets you say "high score" with confidence, or tells you not to.
- **Judge validation.** Hand-review about 100 reports for whether each reference finding was mentioned, and compute agreement with the LLM judge. You could do this yourself in an afternoon.

**Moderate, requiring GPU time**

- **Path-B prose on the in-house cohort, with and without indications.** Running both conditions resolves the indication confound and measures how much indications change the silent-miss rate. This is the analysis most likely to be demanded in review.
- **Generation stability.** Generate three to five reports per study on a representative subset. Report how often a silent miss stays silent across runs. This preempts the Beaulieu-Jones style of critique.

**More ambitious, but it would localize the failure**

- **A Perceiver bottleneck probe.** Train a simple linear probe for the critical labels on the 64 Perceiver latents, and compare it with the same probe on the full encoder tokens. If the information survives the Perceiver, the loss is in the language decoder. If it doesn't, the loss is in compression. Either answer sharpens the mechanism paragraph.
- **Decoder likelihood of the missing finding.** Under teacher forcing, compute the log-probability the decoder assigns to the omitted finding's text, given the visual tokens. If the decoder considers the finding likely but omits it, the failure lies in the concise-output training objective rather than in what the decoder knows. This is optional and elegant, but it is the first thing to drop if time is short.
